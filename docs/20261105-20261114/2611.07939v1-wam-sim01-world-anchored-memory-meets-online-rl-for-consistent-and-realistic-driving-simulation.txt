ABSTRACT
Autoregressive driving world models systematically suffer from structural drift,
identity drift, and quality decay over long rollout horizons due to transient condi-
tioning signals that lack persistent state. We present WAM-Sim, a driving world
model anchored by a persistent hierarchical memory and optimized via online
flow-matching RL. WAM-Sim structures persistent context across three tempo-
ral and semantic scales: pose-aligned structural tokens encoding persistent road
topology, read-only scene tokens supplying geolocated background appearance,
and write-once instance tokens preserving individual traffic participant identities
across occlusions and chunk boundaries. To restore visual sharpness and physical
plausibility degraded by multi-scale conditioning, we introduce an online post-
training framework that optimizes self-generated rollouts via flow-matching RL
guided by hierarchy-aligned visual rewards and a vision-language model physical
judge. The invariant memory empirically confines policy optimization within the
anchored context, helping suppress semantic collapse and reward hacking, while
online RL systematically enforces physical commonsense and fine-grained visual
fidelity. On the nuScenes benchmark, WAM-Sim achieves a superior generation
fidelity (FID=3.47, FVD=25.72) and strong downstream perception performance
(24.16 mAP, 71.23 road mIoU). On minute-scale rollouts of self-collected data,
WAM-Sim substantially mitigates structural and identity drift, preserving high
scene similarity and subject identity coherence across extended horizons. Project
page: https://fudan-generative-vision.github.io/WAM-Sim.

INTRODUCTION
Video world models offer a scalable paradigm for controllable closed-loop simulation in autonomous
driving, synthesizing driving observations conditioned on ego trajectories and agent controls (Hu
et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2024c; Wen et al., 2024; Russell et al., 2025). Despite recent progress,
standard training and evaluation regimes remain predominantly constrained to short temporal clips
and instantaneous point-estimate metrics. As rollouts extend toward minute-scale horizons, gener-
ation systematically degrades through three recurring failure modes: 1) structural drift, where road
geometry and background appearance detach from geographic reality; 2) identity drift, where traf-
fic participants undergo unconstrained appearance, category, or tracking mutations; and 3) quality
decay, where accumulated autoregressive errors recursively compound over time. Although recent
architectures incorporate extended context windows, temporal filtering, or instance masking (Guo
et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025b; Liu et al., 2026; Cheng et al., 2026; Basant et al., 2026), these ca-
pabilities operate in isolation without a persistent and addressable state. We argue that long-horizon
generation failure is not merely a function of limited context window length, but fundamentally
demands persistent, multi-scale memory semantics to anchor identity, appearance and geometry.
In conventional driving world models, conditioning signals such as high-definition (HD) maps, 3D
bounding boxes, and reference frames are provided as transient per-frame or per-chunk inputs.

While transient conditioning constrains immediate frame synthesis, it fails to establish a persis-
tent memory state across extended rollouts. Recent retrieval-augmented models and object-centric
attention mechanisms demonstrate the value of persistent scene context and isolated participant con-
ditioning (Wu et al., 2025b; Xiao et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025; Jia et al., 2025; Seo et al., 2026; Yang
& Zhang, 2026). However, existing methods deploy these mechanisms in isolation, failing to jointly
preserve global route geometry, geolocated scene appearance, and individual object identities un-
der dynamic mid-rollout interventions. Consequently, once generation extends beyond the initial
context window, persistent states must be reconstructed from noisy self-generated histories, causing
errors to compound over time. Overcoming this bottleneck requires a unified memory architecture
that represents road structure, environmental appearance, and participant identity as distinct visual
tokens, maintains each according to its temporal persistence, and retrieves each through mechanisms
matched to its spatio-temporal validity domain.
To address this challenge, we present a driving world model anchored by a persistent world anchored
memory. The memory organizes the driving world across three temporal and semantic scales: static
structural tokens encoded from HD lane maps serve as persistent geometric anchors; read-only scene
tokens retrieved from geolocated databases supply background appearance via temporally routed
cross-attention; and instance tokens registered in a write-once appearance bank preserve individual
object identities across chunk boundaries and occlusions through instance-masked cross-attention.
Although this explicit memory substrate substantially mitigates structural and identity drift, injecting
such dense, multi-scale conditioning signals inevitably shifts the generator off its pretrained distri-
bution, compromising native visual sharpness and multi-agent physical plausibility. To overcome
this trade-off, we introduce Drive DiffusionNFT, an online post-training framework that optimizes
self-generated rollouts via flow-matching reinforcement learning guided by hierarchy-aligned vi-
sual rewards and a physical judge. Beyond their individual roles, we empirically observe a practical
synergy between explicit memory persistence and online policy optimization: the invariant memory
state appears to confine the effective policy optimization space, coinciding with reduced semantic
drift and reward hacking during recursive rollouts, while online RL systematically restores fine-
grained visual fidelity and enforces physical commonsense without compromising global stability.
Extensive evaluations on the standard nuScenes benchmark and a self-collected challenging long-
horizon dataset demonstrate the effectiveness of WAM-Sim. On nuScenes, WAM-Sim achieves
superior generation fidelity (FID = 3.47, FVD = 25.72) alongside strong downstream percep-
tion performance (24.16 mAP, 71.23 road mIoU). On minute-scale rollouts of self-collected data,

RELATED WORK
Driving World Models.
Driving video generation has evolved from action- and trajectory-
conditioned models (Hu et al., 2023; Russell et al., 2025; Gao et al., 2024c) to layout- and map-
conditioned video synthesis (Wang et al., 2024a; Wen et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2024b;a; Zhao et al.,
2025; Wang et al., 2024b; Li et al., 2024; Mei et al., 2024), autoregressive long-horizon genera-
tion (Jia et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025; Guo et al., 2024), geometry-aware 4D
modeling (Yang et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025; Guo et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2024b), and driving-
specialized foundation models (NVIDIA, 2025a; Ren et al., 2025; Ni et al., 2025; Basant et al.,
2026). InstaDrive (Yang et al., 2026) propagates instance features to preserve identity, yet treats
such evidence as transient per-chunk conditioning; HorizonDrive (Zhang et al., 2026) extends roll-
outs toward minute-scale horizons via streaming context. Across these methods, maps, boxes, and
retrieved context remain transient, with no persistent, addressable state binding geographic location
and instance identities; we replace transient conditioning with a world anchored memory.
Memory and Long-Horizon Consistency. Persistence in interactive world models is achieved by
attention caches (Valevski et al., 2025; Decart & Etched, 2024; Parker-Holder et al., 2024; Google
DeepMind, 2025), retrieval over past observations (Xiao et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025;
Zhao et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2025b), or geographic grounding (Deng et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025;
Jia et al., 2025). Orthogonal efforts extend or compress context (Qiu et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2024;
Henschel et al., 2025; Zhang & Agrawala, 2025; Huang et al., 2025a; Oshima et al., 2025) and
mitigate exposure bias (Chen et al., 2024a; Yin et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2025b; Liu et al., 2026).

METHOD
As shown in Figure 2, we present a long-horizon driving world model (Section 3.1) built on two
mutually reinforcing components: a persistent world anchored memory that anchors generation to
a real geographic location and to per-instance identities (Section 3.2), and Drive DiffusionNFT, an
online post-training stage that optimizes visual and physical realism on self-generated rollouts (Sec-
tion 3.3). The two components are synergistic by design: the memory state delimits the manifold
within which reward optimization operates, while the rewards are aligned with the memory hierar-
chy, so that the policy improves precisely the properties that memory persists.

3.2
WORLD ANCHORED MEMORY
A driving world retains information at multiple scales—road topology, geolocated scene context, and
traffic-participant identities—which differ in semantics, spatial scope, and temporal persistence. We
therefore formalize the world state as a hierarchical memory tuple M =
 Mstruct, Mscene, Minst

where each tier is maintained under a dedicated persistence mechanism and retrieved through an
operator aligned with its spatio-temporal validity domain.
Memory Token Extraction. We extract memory tokens across three distinct semantic and temporal
scales. First, structural tokens capture static road topology by projecting the per-frame HD-map
sequence into the latent video grid via a dedicated map embedding layer. Second, scene tokens
represent persistent background appearance; we partition the rollout of Ctotal frames into Nb =
⌈Ctotal/Tb⌉non-overlapping temporal segments of Tb frames (one chunk per segment by default)
and, for each segment, retrieve the nearest reference image from the geolocated database using
the segment’s global ego pose, subject to a spatial radius rmax and a camera-heading alignment
constraint; if no reference satisfies both criteria, the segment reuses the reference of its preceding
segment. Each reference image is VAE-encoded into L patch tokens Sn ∈RL×D, forming the
unified scene memory matrix S = ConcatNb
n=1 Sn ∈RNbL×D. Third, instance tokens encode object
identity. For each tracked instance, after filtering observations by the bounding-box area range
[ρmin, ρmax], we divide the remaining frames into at most Kf temporal groups and select the largest
observation from each. The selected crops are VAE-encoded and projected into Bi ∈RK×D, where
K = KfKp and Kp is the number of tokens per crop. A validity vector νi masks padded slots.
Hierarchical Persistence. The three memory tiers operate under persistence mechanisms strictly
aligned with their temporal dynamics. Structural memory is trajectory-aligned: the global road
topology remains invariant, while its per-frame HD-map representation dynamically transforms rel-
ative to the ego pose. Scene memory is geographically grounded and read-only: geolocated refer-
ences in Mscene remain static throughout generation and cannot be overwritten by self-generated
content, thereby anchoring environmental textures, architecture, and road surfaces to real-world co-
ordinates. Instance memory follows a write-once policy: upon an agent’s initial appearance, its
visual tokens Bi are cached in a track-keyed bank Minst = {τi 7→Bi} indexed by non-parametric
track identifiers τi. Locking Bi against subsequent overwrites prevents autoregressive error accu-
mulation from corrupting object identities, substantially improving re-identification across extended
occlusions and chunk boundaries.
Hierarchical Memory Injection.
Rather than exposing all memory tokens to every video to-
ken, our hierarchical routing progressively conditions a shared video representation with geometry-,
environment-, and identity-level information at their corresponding validity domains.
Structural memory provides trajectory-aligned geometric information. Since the HD-map represen-
tation is spatially aligned with the noisy video latent, it is incorporated as an additive condition,
X(0) = Zt + Mstruct, where Zt denotes the noisy video latent at flow timestep t. This establishes
the geometry-aware video representation on which the subsequent memory routing operates. Scene
memory operates at the temporal-segment level. For video token j, let s(j) denote its corresponding
segment. The routing mask is defined as Rscene
j,(n−1)L+r = 0 when s(j) = n and otherwise −∞,
where r ∈{1, . . . , L}, ensuring that each token retrieves scene information only from its associated
temporal segment. Scene-memory retrieval is performed on the current geometry-conditioned video
representation, producing an updated representation that incorporates the corresponding environ-
mental context. Instance memory further specializes this shared representation within object-specific
spatio-temporal regions. Given N tracked instances, we rasterize their bounding boxes onto the
video-token grid to form an instance-membership matrix Π ∈{0, 1}Nv×N, where Nv = CH′W ′
denotes the number of video tokens per chunk and Πj,i = 1 if and only if token j belongs to instance
i. Only foreground tokens covered by tracked instances participate in the instance-memory branch,
while background tokens bypass it. Each foreground token interacts only with video tokens belong-

ing to the same instance and retrieves appearance information only from the corresponding entry in
the instance memory bank. Cross-instance connections and invalid memory tokens are masked out.
Importantly, instance-memory retrieval operates on the video representation already conditioned by
both structural and scene information.
The resulting formulation constitutes a coarse-to-fine hierarchical conditioning process over a shared
video representation: structural memory establishes trajectory-aligned geometry, scene memory in-
troduces segment-specific environmental context, and instance memory preserves object-specific ap-
pearance within tracked regions. Although their validity domains differ, the three memories jointly
form a single hierarchical memory system rather than independent parallel branches.

EXPERIMENTAL SETUPS
Implementation Details.
Our generator is a 3D DiT initialized from pretrained OmniDreams
weights and trained in two stages: World-Anchored Memory pre-training and Drive DiffusionNFT
post-training. Stage I sequentially optimizes trajectory-aligned structural conditioning, scene mem-
ory, and instance memory for 130k, 15k, and 5k steps, respectively. All use batch size 8 and context
parallelism degree 8, with learning rates of 1 × 10−5, 3 × 10−5, and 3 × 10−4. Starting from the
full Stage I checkpoint, Stage II applies online flow-matching RL to self-generated rollouts using
G = 16 and LoRA adapters (rank 256, LR 1 × 10−5). We use AdamW throughout and train all
experiments on 32 Ascend 910B NPUs.

we evaluate on nuScenes, which provides 850 20-second driving scenes (700 for training, 150 for
validation) with synchronized multi-view video, 3D bounding boxes, and HD-map annotations. To
evaluate long-horizon stability, we train our model on a self-collected dataset comprising 121.8K
multi-modal clips (∼1,000 hours) across diverse Chinese urban, rural, and highway environments.
Each training sample includes 10 FPS front-view video (704×1280), 3D bounding boxes with per-
sistent track IDs, rendered HD-map topologies, GPS coordinates linked to a geolocated visual refer-
ence database, and structured captions. The corresponding benchmark evaluation set comprises 350
curated long-horizon clips featuring adverse weather, complex maneuvers, vulnerable road users,
and extended occlusion–re-emergence events (representative scenarios shown in Fig. 6).
Evaluation Metrics. We evaluate generation across four core dimensions: 1) visual fidelity via
FID, FVD, and VBench Quality Score; 2) long-horizon consistency via Subject Coherence (instance
identity) and Scene Similarity (background stability); 3) object & physical realism via detection-
gated Subject Fidelity and VLM-guided Physical Plausibility; and 4) downstream perception on
nuScenes via 3D detection (mAP, NDS) and BEV segmentation mIoU (road, vehicle).

ABLATION AND DISCUSSION
Architecture Component Ablation. Table 3 disentangles the complementary roles of Scene Mem-
ory and Instance Memory on the self-collected dataset. Individually, Scene Memory anchors global
environmental context, boosting Scene Similarity from 0.852 to 0.904 and reducing FVD from 81.64
to 76.68 over the memory-free baseline. Conversely, Instance Memory strengthens fine-grained
identity persistence, elevating Subject Coherence to 0.680, though its standalone FVD degrades
to 85.55 due to a lack of global structural bounds. Jointly integrating both tiers achieves optimal
performance—yielding the lowest FVD, peak Subject Coherence, and Scene Similarity.
Scene Memory. We evaluate the robustness and appearance-layout disentanglement of scene mem-
ory. Quantitatively, introducing reference dropout at inference demonstrates both active reliance
and strong fault tolerance (Table 4); even at p = 0.3, Scene Similarity decays marginally from
0.910 to 0.904. Qualitatively, Figure 7(left) demonstrates that scene memory effectively disentan-
gles persistent spatial layouts from transient environmental conditions: when conditioned on a dusk
reference image (“Background”), WAM-Sim faithfully preserves the underlying architectural ge-
ometry and road layout throughout target daytime rollouts (“Ours”). Conversely, omitting scene
memory (“w/o”) leads to severe structural drift and hallucinated background geometry.

5
CONCLUSION
We presented WAM-Sim, a long-horizon driving world model that systematically mitigates struc-
tural drift, identity mutation, and quality decay. By unifying a persistent world anchored mem-
ory—anchoring road topology, geolocated appearance, and instance identity—with Drive Diffu-
sionNFT online post-training, WAM-Sim establishes a persistent world state while enforcing visual
sharpness and physical plausibility. Benchmark evaluations on nuScenes and self-collected driving
datasets demonstrate superior generation fidelity, robust downstream perception, and temporal sta-
bility over extended rollouts. This synergy between invariant memory manifolds and reward-guided
policy optimization offers a promising paradigm toward closed-loop simulation.

EXTENDED RELATED WORK
Driving World Models. Controllable driving video generation was pioneered by action-conditioned
world models such as GAIA-1 and GAIA-2 (Hu et al., 2023; Russell et al., 2025), high-fidelity
trajectory-conditioned prediction in Vista (Gao et al., 2024c), and multi-view generators conditioned
on BEV layouts, HD maps, and 3D bounding boxes (Wang et al., 2024a; Wen et al., 2024; Gao et al.,
2024b;a; Zhao et al., 2025). Autoregressive token-based approaches extend such models to longer
horizons (Jia et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025), while geometry-aware methods im-
prove spatial and 4D consistency (Yang et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025; Guo et al., 2025; Chen et al.,
2024b). Two recent trends are most relevant to our setting. First, world foundation models spe-
cialized for driving—Cosmos and Cosmos-Drive-Dreams (NVIDIA, 2025a; Ren et al., 2025), and
most recently OmniDreams (Basant et al., 2026)—achieve photorealistic, interactive, real-time roll-
outs under world-scenario control, with OmniDreams attaining minute-long closed-loop simulation
through a streaming KV cache and self-forcing-style distillation. Second, a line of work explicitly
targets long driving sequences: InfinityDrive introduces memory injection and retention for minute-
scale driving video (Guo et al., 2024). Despite this progress, maps, boxes, and retrieved context are
re-specified per chunk and therefore constrain only instantaneous generation; what persists across
chunks—an addressable state binding a real location and per-instance identities—remains implicit
(e.g., in attention caches) rather than explicit and controllable. We upgrade transient conditioning to
a persistent world anchored memory while retaining minute-scale intervenability.
Memory and Long-Horizon Consistency. Interactive world models increasingly rely on explicit
memory. Genie 3 exhibits emergent scene memory over minute-scale rollouts but, by its own ac-
count, cannot reproduce real-world locations with geographic accuracy (Google DeepMind, 2025).
Retrieval-based memories index past observations for consistency: WorldMem maintains a retriev-
able bank indexed by camera pose (Xiao et al., 2025), VMem indexes past views by surfel ge-
ometry (Li et al., 2025), Spatia preserves an updatable 3D point cloud maintained with visual
SLAM (Zhao et al., 2026), and video world models with long-term spatial memory support view
re-identification over revisits (Wu et al., 2025b). Complementary to these generic mechanisms, ge-
olocated generation grounds synthesis in real places: Streetscapes synthesizes street views of an on-
the-fly city rather than actual locations (Deng et al., 2024); TeraSim-World grounds safety-critical
data synthesis at arbitrary coordinates but channels real-world appearance through a VLM text bot-
tleneck restricted to static elements (Wang et al., 2025); and, concurrently with our work, Spatial-
RAG-AD retrieves geographic imagery along the ego route and injects it as persistent cross-attention
context into driving video DiTs (Jia et al., 2025). On the training side, long-horizon methods either
extend context windows (FreeNoise (Qiu et al., 2024), FIFO-Diffusion (Kim et al., 2024), Stream-
ingT2V (Henschel et al., 2025)) or close the exposure-bias gap with self-generated rollouts, from
Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., 2024a) and CausVid (Yin et al., 2025) to Self-Forcing (Huang et al.,
2025b) and Rolling Forcing (Liu et al., 2026); OmniDreams concurrently adopts self-forcing distil-
lation for real-time driving rollouts (Basant et al., 2026). Instance-level consistency within single
clips is pursued by identity-preserving driving generation (Yang & Zhang, 2026) and reference-
image subject consistency methods (Shen et al., 2026). Existing works, however, provide either
scene-level memory without real geographic anchors, geographic grounding without a persistent
cross-chunk memory state, or instance consistency without occlusion-surviving, write-once identity
evidence under counterfactual manipulation. We unify the three: a coordinate-indexed scene mem-
ory reused for the entire rollout together with a write-once appearance bank keyed by track identity,
retrieved through banked instance-masked attention at chunk boundaries and upon re-emergence
after occlusion.
Reinforcement Learning for Visual Generation. RL-based alignment of diffusion and flow mod-
els has progressed from policy-gradient fine-tuning of denoising MDPs (DDPO (Black et al., 2023);
DPOK and Diffusion-DPO for image alignment (Fan et al., 2023; Wallace et al., 2023); VADER for
video (Prabhudesai et al., 2024)), through video-scale preference optimization (SkyReels-V2 (Chen
et al., 2025)), to online group-relative policy optimization for flow matching: Flow-GRPO intro-
duced ODE-to-SDE exploration (Liu et al., 2025), DanceGRPO extended it to general visual gen-
eration (Xue et al., 2025), Phys-AR optimizes physical velocity-error rewards for video genera-
tion (Lin et al., 2025), and RLVR-World trains world models with RL (Wu et al., 2025a); reward-
guided optimization has likewise been used for identity-preserving image-to-video generation (Shen
et al., 2026). On the evaluation side, physical commonsense benchmarks such as VideoPhy (Bansal
et al., 2024), PhysGame, and T2VPhysBench diagnose violations without training, while VLM
15


judges including Cosmos-Reason1 (NVIDIA, 2025b) and VBench-2.0 Motion Rationality estab-
lish that vision-language models can score physical plausibility; driving-specific suites such as
DrivingGen (Zhou et al., 2026) and WorldLens (Liang et al., 2025) provide comprehensive but
point-estimate evaluations on fixed short horizons. To our knowledge, we are the first to combine
online flow-matching RL with driving-specific rewards—hierarchical visual fidelity at scene and
instance levels together with a VLM-based physical plausibility judge—optimized directly on self-
generated multi-chunk rollouts, and the first to evaluate long-horizon driving world models with
quality–horizon decay curves, counterfactual ID-surgery, and mid-rollout intervention protocols.

HUMAN EVALUATION
Experimental Setup and Protocol. We evaluate human perceptual preference on 60 represen-
tative driving scenarios sampled from the test set. Fifteen independent expert assessors with back-
grounds in autonomous driving evaluate WAM-Sim against four baselines: UniMLVG, OpenDWM–
CTSD3.5, HorizonDrive, and OmniDreams. To eliminate bias, all method identities are double-
blinded, and video presentation order and spatial placement are randomized. Assessors perform two
complementary tasks: (i) forced-choice pairwise preference voting (Win, Tie, or Loss) for side-by-
side video pairs, and (ii) five-point Mean Opinion Score (MOS) rating across four core dimensions:
Visual Fidelity (perceptual quality and temporal coherence), Identity Consistency (long-horizon par-
ticipant appearance preservation across occlusions), Physical Realism (plausibility of agent dynam-
ics and road interactions), and Location Anchoring (alignment with spatial and geographic context).
Score Aggregation and Inter-Rater Reliability. Pairwise preference judgments are aggregated per
scenario via majority voting across the 15 assessors to determine scenario-level outcomes. MOS
ratings are averaged per scenario across assessors and subsequently macro-averaged over all 60
scenarios. Inter-rater reliability across the 15 judges is validated using Fleiss’ κ on categorical
pairwise preferences, demonstrating strong agreement across all baseline comparisons: κUniMLVG =
0.927, κOpenDWM = 0.864, κHorizonDrive = 0.939, and κOmniDreams = 0.972.
Quantitative Results and Statistical Analysis. As summarized in Table 7, WAM-Sim substan-

zonDrive (43/1/16), and 86.7% against OmniDreams (52/0/8). Furthermore, WAM-Sim secures
the highest MOS across all four perceptual dimensions (Fidelity: 4.30, Identity: 4.40, Physics: 4.50,
Location: 4.50). To assess statistical significance, we conduct two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank
tests (α = 0.05) on paired scenario-level MOS values (Table 8). WAM-Sim achieves statistically
significant gains over UniMLVG, OpenDWM–CTSD3.5, and OmniDreams across all dimensions
(p < 10−9). Compared to HorizonDrive (the strongest baseline), WAM-Sim yields statistically sig-
nificant improvements in Identity (p = 7.65 × 10−4), Physics (p = 4.32 × 10−8), and Location
(p = 3.41 × 10−6), confirming that explicit world memory and online RL post-training primarily
enhance long-horizon temporal consistency, physical plausibility, and spatial anchoring.

IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS
We present the architectural specifications, stage-wise training workflow, and reward formulations
of WAM-Sim. The framework is trained in four progressive stages: structural control pre-training,
scene-memory alignment, instance-memory registration, and online reinforcement post-training.
Key model configurations and training parameters are summarized in Tables 11, 12, and 10.
C.1
MODEL CONFIGURATION
WAM-Sim is built on a 28-block 3D Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone with a hidden dimen-
sion of 2048 and 16 attention heads (Dhead = 128), conditioned on text embeddings from a frozen
Qwen2.5-VL-7B encoder. Input videos at 704 × 1280 resolution (93 frames at 10 FPS) are encoded
by a Wan2.1 Video VAE with 8× spatial and 4× temporal compression into a 24 × 88 × 160 latent
grid. Applying 2×2×1 spatio-temporal patchification yields 84,480 video tokens per chunk, modu-
lated via 3D RoPE and timestep-conditioned adaLN-zero layers. As summarized in Table 11, Hierar-
chical World Memory conditions this shared sequence across three scale-specific tiers: (i) Structural
Memory injects VAE-aligned HD map latents as additive conditioning to establish trajectory-aligned
geometry; (ii) Scene Memory retrieves one geolocated reference frame (P tokens) per temporal seg-
ment, injecting background appearance via segment-masked cross-attention with zero-initialized
adaLN gating; and (iii) Instance Memory extracts multi-frame appearance crops (Kf = 4 frames,
Kp = 64 tokens per crop, capping at 32 active instances) into a write-once track-keyed bank, updat-
ing foreground tokens via instance-masked attention while bypassing background regions.

TRAINING CONFIGURATION
WAM-Sim is trained via a progressive four-stage curriculum that incrementally introduces struc-
tural guidance, hierarchical memory routing, and online post-training without destabilizing the
pretrained backbone. Stage I pre-trains structural control for 130k steps (LR = 1 × 10−5) us-

cross-attention. Stage III optimizes instance memory for 5k steps (LR = 3 × 10−4) to register
write-once appearance tokens into the track-keyed memory bank. Finally, Stage IV executes online
post-training via Drive DiffusionNFT (LR = 1 × 10−5) with LoRA adapters to align self-generated
rollouts against multi-scale visual and physical rewards. All stages utilize the AdamW optimizer
with constant learning rates, 8× context parallelism, and bfloat16 precision; complete optimization
hyperparameters are summarized in Table 12.

DATASET CONSTRUCTION
Training a long-horizon driving world model requires more than large-scale video data. The model
must learn temporally consistent visual dynamics together with structured geometric conditions,
persistent object identities, and semantic scene descriptions. We therefore construct a large-scale
in-house autonomous-driving dataset and apply a multi-stage processing pipeline to transform raw
driving logs into structured training samples. This section describes the data sources, construction
pipeline, and final training and evaluation sets.
D.1
DATA SOURCE
Our empirical dataset is constructed from a large-scale, in-house autonomous driving corpus gath-
ered across geographically diverse regions in China. The raw recordings are organized hierarchically
into continuous scenes, each comprising temporally aligned clips with synchronized multi-modal
sensor streams and structured annotations. Every frame contains a timestamped front-view RGB
image (704 × 1280 at 10 FPS) paired with synchronized vehicle state trajectories, sensor calibra-
tion profiles, high-precision LiDAR point clouds, per-frame semantic segmentation masks, and 3D
agent bounding boxes parameterized by persistent tracking IDs, positions, and orientations in the
ego-vehicle coordinate system. Together, these multi-modal streams provide the dense geometric,
visual, and instance-level groundings necessary for multi-scale world model training.
D.2

Raw driving logs inherently contain sensor anomalies, extreme motion, and misaligned annotations
unsuitable for world-model training. To extract high-fidelity, structured training samples, we execute
a systematic curation and annotation pipeline (Fig. 10).
We first enforce data quality through a three-stage filtering protocol. Stage I: Integrity Filtering
discards sequences exhibiting dropped frames, exposure failures, or temporal discontinuities, pre-
venting the generator from learning low-level sensor artifacts as scene dynamics. Stage II: Kine-
matic and Density Filtering mitigates motion blur and severe occlusion ambiguity by removing clips
with average ego-velocities exceeding 45 km/h or heavily crowded scenes containing more than 20
annotated bounding boxes per frame. Stage III: Cross-Modal Alignment ensures precise geomet-
ric fidelity between spatial annotations and visual observations. We project HD-map elements and

E
EVALUATION AND METRIC FORMULATIONS
E.1
QUALITY-HORIZON EVALUATION
We evaluate long-horizon generation stability at three rollout durations, H = {10, 30, 60} s. Starting
from the same initial conditioning, each method is rolled out using the same autoregressive chunk-
wise generation protocol. For each target horizon h ∈H, we retain the generated sequence from the

E.4
RL EVALUATION METRICS
Subject Fidelity. We measure whether the conditioned dynamic subjects are detectable in the gen-
erated video with a GT-gated detector protocol. Conditioning boxes (per-frame tight AABBs of the
conditioned pedestrians, cyclists, cars and vehicles) are taken from the ground-truth annotations. A
YOLO26s detector trained on BDD100k is run on every frame of the generated video; each condi-
tioning box is greedily matched to the highest-IoU same-class detection. To exclude annotation and
visibility ambiguity from the denominator, the identical detection-and-matching procedure is run on
the ground-truth video, and only instances the detector recovers in the GT frames count as valid.
Physical Plausibility. We use Qwen3.5-9B as a no-reference VLM judge to evaluate pedestrian
motion plausibility. Each video is evaluated with two binary questions covering motion anatomy
and limb integrity. We use the normalized probability of “yes” at the first generated token rather
than a sampled binary response. Specifically, the probability is computed by renormalizing the
logits over the predefined yes/no token variants. The final score is the mean P(yes) across the two
questions. Only videos with clearly visible pedestrians are included in this evaluation.
Physical-Plausibility Prompts
Motion Anatomy: Do all walking or running pedestrians show perfectly natural, anatomically plausible
limb movement throughout the entire video, with zero unnatural joint motion and zero abnormal limb
trajectories? (yes or no)
Limb Integrity: For moving pedestrians, do their arms and legs remain clearly distinguishable throughout
the motion, without limbs merging into the body, merging with each other, disappearing, or becoming
visually entangled? (yes or no)

LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK
Despite its strong long-horizon consistency, WAM-Sim exhibits three primary limitations. First,
memory retrieval latency and attention memory footprint scale linearly with the number of tracked
25


foreground instances, introducing computational overhead in hyper-dense traffic scenes contain-
ing over 100 concurrent agents. Second, the write-once instance appearance bank assumes rigid
visual properties; consequently, extreme non-rigid state transitions (e.g., severe vehicle damage dur-
ing collisions or drastic open/close deformations of commercial truck beds) are constrained by the
initial-observation tokens. Third, background scene retrieval depends on geolocated reference avail-
ability, which may degrade to ungrounded autoregressive generation in unmapped or highly dynamic
construction zones.
To address these challenges, future iterations will pursue three key research directions: 1) Integrat-
ing explicit 3D visual representations, such as online-updated Gaussian Splatting or neural radiance
fields, into the memory substrate to enable unified multi-view spatial consistency and continuous
geometry-aware retrieval under counterfactual maneuvers. 2) Formulating a parametric write-update
memory mechanism that selectively incorporates visual state changes across significant illumina-
tion or topological shifts while preserving fundamental instance identity invariants. 3) Developing
dynamic instance-grouping and memory-compression protocols to bound memory complexity in
extreme ultra-dense urban traffic scenarios.
G
ETHICS, BROADER IMPACT, AND REPRODUCIBILITY ASSURANCE
WAM-Sim advances autonomous driving safety by enabling controllable, zero-cost edge-case sim-
ulation over extended horizons. While high-fidelity video generation presents potential dual-use
risks regarding synthetic media misuse, these concerns can be effectively mitigated through digital
watermarking protocols and restricted access frameworks for generative models.
To ensure full reproducibility, we release our complete training and evaluation code, model check-
points, and dataset processing pipelines. Detailed architectural configurations, optimization hyper-
parameters, and hardware environments are thoroughly documented in Appendices C and D.
